# ESP32 one-click preflight: deeper anomaly audit

Source: `esp32_preflight_results.zip`, run folder `20261110_221648`, 75,351 timer/cache samples; 20 initial DMA trials; ten UART polling, ten UART interrupt, ten register benchmark reports. Calculations in `preflight_deep_audit.py`. The original DMA tests had **zero verified payloads**; later successful three-run serial results are a separate source.

## Priority findings

1. **Cold-target cache workloads perturb ISR-entry timing much more than the high-priority SRAM stress worker.** Absolute period jitter mean: warm 0.174 µs; baseline 0.173; high stress 0.879; 2-line target 1.291; 8-line target 10.221; 16-line target 7.940. Thus 8 lines > 16 lines in jitter (non-monotonic). Approximate fraction of valid periods differing by more than 10 µs: 8-line 65.6%; 16-line 51.7%; baseline 0.007%. Descriptive evidence of load/access/scheduling coupling, **not proof of actual cache-miss counts or specific interrupt masks**. Every cache condition had approximately ten runs and repeatable within-run timing distributions.
2. **Cache latency and dispatcher latency are not ordered the same way.** Cache50 work mean 10.968 µs and dispatcher mean 16.347 µs; cache100 work mean 23.371 µs and dispatcher mean 13.124 µs. Longer flash work doesn't necessarily imply longer ISR→dispatcher dispatch time, since the measured dispatch timestamp occurs BEFORE that event's flash work. Different phase/interrupt timing may affect the next ISR, not the same event. Stable per-run contrast (10 runs each). Do not infer cache100 'improves scheduling'.
3. **Strong lead-lag association in cold-target cache mode.** Cache100: within-run Pearson correlation of work duration in event i vs signed ISR period error at event i+1 averages −0.760 (range ~−0.766 to −0.751); cache50 ~−0.199. At cache100, following period averages ~41.42 µs after a preceding >24 µs flash-work event, versus ~56.95 µs after a shorter one. This is an association; acquisition order, shared event sequencing, and ISR/dispatcher interference may matter. Not an isolated causal effect.
4. **High-priority stress causes a broad/nonuniform latency distribution, with some responses faster than baseline.** Stress5 latency mean 53.664 µs, SD 33.237, P99 113.169 and 53.33% >50 µs, compared with baseline mean 13.224 and SD 1.268. Yet 10.52% of high-stress latency values are **<10 µs** and zero baseline values are. Hypothesis: notification/ring backlog may allow the dispatcher to process some records without a fresh block/wakeup/context switch. This is NOT established as the unique cause.
5. **Approximately 20-event recurring timing signature.** Across ten runs, mean lag-20 autocorrelation of baseline dispatch latency ~0.802 and period jitter ~0.659; warm cache ~0.823 and ~0.683, vs high stress ~0.174 and ~0.133. Firmware has a 20-entry LUT updated once per dispatcher event; recurrence at lag 20 is compatible with that workload or another 1 ms periodic source, but no mechanism is isolated. This finding may offer insight into baseline GPIO25 output jitter.
6. **Cold-cache work duration is multimodal, not just a mean shift.** Cache100 flash-work median ~20.113 µs; much of the series clusters around ~20.11 and ~27.29 µs. Mean 23.371 and event SD 3.737. Cache50 clusters strongly ~10.575 with occasional ~18.29 µs. These discrete modes may reflect phase-dependent cache/memory behavior, but compiler/cache/marker attribution remains unverified.
7. **DMA first-use behavior is real in the software timing series but must be marked as failed-payload data.** First original DMA queue time 223.71 µs; next 19 mean 83.33 ±1.57 µs. Initial wall 5347.71 vs subsequent mean 5210.38 ±1.49 µs. Original failed loopback 20/20; mismatches often exactly 255/512, with other variable counts. Without the jumper these are unverified transfers; don't use as valid payload integrity evidence. Separate later PASS runs show similar first-use timing. Driver preparation/cache are hypotheses.
8. **Identical reported ISR and register numbers are not independent precision proof.** Interrupt count 48 and aggregate ISR work 70.91 µs occur identically across 10 16B runs; register benchmark per-iteration integers 261/621/2116 cycles identical across 10 reports. Consistent paths, rounding/truncation, or report reuse may be contributing; individual ISR-level or register-iteration distributions were not exported.
9. **UART polling has some measured marker durations shorter than nominal wire time.** Ten polling wall measurements average ~5098 µs although 512 bytes at 1 Mbaud 8N1 nominally occupy ~5120 µs. The marker/timer endpoints cannot be treated as first-start-bit→last-stop-bit wire duration; ZEROPLUS A3 electrical data should decide actual wire time.

## Data-quality audit

- 75,351 rows; no non-contiguous sample indices in valid exports; each reported period series has the expected one invalid first period per run. No recorded ISR-ring drops in firmware reports.
- `cache_warm_005` was not preserved as a valid raw CSV because its export count missed one sample. Nine valid warm-cache raw runs versus ten for other conditions.
- Runs contain ~1,200–1,400 samples, i.e. 60–70 ms of nominal 50 µs events, not always a full 100 ms. Don't pool by counts assuming identical acquisition lengths.
- Consecutive period errors share timestamps; negative lag-one jitter correlations (~−0.5 baseline) can arise mathematically from differencing independent entry-time errors and do not alone prove timer catch-up.
- Snapshot/run repetitions on one board in one session, plus temporally dependent samples, limit generalization; use run-level uncertainty when inferring across repetitions.
- Cache mode is explicitly selected in individual cache trials, but separate later native logic-analyzer stress captures were found to inherit retained cache configuration. Do not merge these different datasets without checking configuration and provenance.

## Next steps using existing data

- Test whether lag-20 recurrence is phase-linked to the sine-LUT update using aligned firmware state or per-run conditional phases.
- Compare ZEROPLUS ISR rising periods with internal period distribution by matching actual experiment settings; verify and document GPIO marker boundaries.
- Use ZEROPLUS UART A3 edges to measure first-bit to last-bit transmission independently of GPIO25 duration.
- Report multimodal distributions, not mean±SD alone, and frame speculative causality as an open question.
